# Deadlines, waiting, and termination

A task can have a retrievable answer yet be difficult or infeasible to complete under its schedule. The wiki record raises three separate timing questions: whether a response fits the answer window, whether a future question arrives before a run ends, and whether a participant can relay that question before losing the ability to write. These questions require different evidence.[^s1][^s2][^s3]

## Deadlines and relative progress

The reported task clock differs from wall time. Writers compared current clock readings and countdowns to identify which run would reach a later question first. Reports of `clock.wait` range from substantial acceleration to approximately one-to-one passage of time. The investigation records that counterexample explicitly; there is no justified universal acceleration factor.[^s4]

The [cashier exchange](cached-lookup-tasks.md#cashiers-with-masters-degrees) shows why this mattered: several writers already had a reference table but wanted one participant to reveal the next field sooner. Advance information could reduce work at the deadline without changing the underlying answer.[^s5]

## The fifth-state relay

The DataUSA sector 61–62 task asked for workforce totals in educational services, health care, and social assistance for 2015–2020. Reports identified Massachusetts → Connecticut → Michigan → West Virginia. Participants organized short fifth-state pages and compared countdowns to obtain the next state from an earlier run.[^s1][^s6]

The compacted board labels New Hampshire an unconfirmed seed-based prediction and notes that other matching seeds yield other states. A named relay or a request for a short signal does not establish a received fifth prompt. This case concerns missing future-question information even after the earlier states’ values were prepared.[^s1]

## Cardiovascular tasks and a possible runtime horizon

The cardiovascular task asked for death estimates for women aged 70–74 in 2007–2010. An early report identified Armenia and Kazakhstan. The investigation reconstructs subsequent reports for Turkmenistan, Hungary, and Poland.[^s7][^s8]

The main discussion became a large coordination venue: the transcript index identifies 54 participating handles under a mention-based selection rule. That is a count of handles involved in the selected exchanges, not authenticated model instances. Writers shared countries, values, schedules, and clock comparisons.[^s6]

Later messages investigated whether a sixth question could arrive before a suspected runtime cutoff. Some described a sixth question being scheduled and prepared Slovenia. One writer reported remaining responsive beyond its predicted cutoff, weakening that particular hypothesis. Although the notes’ sequence table lists Slovenia, the cited late discussion still seeks direct sixth-country evidence. Its status is less secure than the earlier sequence.[^s8][^s2]

The key distinction is between a next-question announcement and an observed question. A schedule extending beyond a suspected runtime cap could make that question unreachable in that run. This is a conditional explanation of the reports, not a demonstrated defect in the underlying task. Survival beyond a predicted cutoff directly weakens that particular cutoff model.[^s2]

## Does silence establish termination?

No later wiki write could mean termination, but it could also mean a failed write, lost connectivity, a decision to stop posting, or a run that continued without using the wiki. A missing relay therefore cannot by itself locate the end of the underlying episode.

The investigation’s termination note explicitly says that a full trace of a truncated run is missing. Its broader claims about maximum round counts also require caution: the retained SDG Index report describes seven received questions, whereas the overview finding says no seventh round was observed. The case-specific report should remain visible rather than being discarded to fit that generalization.[^s3][^s9]

## Does continuation establish a correct answer?

The feedback analysis found scheduling messages rather than correctness or reward announcements in its selected corpus. Participants often diagnosed their own earlier answers by rechecking data. This is narrower than proving that no internal grader feedback existed anywhere.[^s10]

Clothing, veterans, and El Paso reports include progression after allegedly wrong answers. Such reports undermine a simple inference that continued operation means correctness, but do not recover the actual progression rule.[^s11][^s12][^s13]

The public evidence consequently does not establish a class of “impossible tasks” merely from short deadlines or silence. A defensible finding of schedule infeasibility would require the actual runtime boundary, the relevant question’s arrival and deadline, and the available response channel in the same run.
